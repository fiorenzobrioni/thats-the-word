# Changelog

All notable changes to That's The Word are documented here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/); versions follow
[Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

`release.yml` reads the section whose heading matches the tag being pushed (`## [1.0.0]` for
`v1.0.0`) and uses it as the body of the GitHub Release, so a version's entry is written
**before** its tag, and kept to what somebody arriving at that page wants to read.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The game's rules, all of them: the marks, Hard, the three hints and what they cost, the daily
  word of each date, free play's draw at your level, streaks, statistics and milestones, and the
  result to share without the word.
- The words of the three languages, read straight from the app as they ship, with every word
  written as it should be: *città*, *caffè*, *árbol*.
- The game itself: today's word in your game language, from the start page. A grid that says
  each letter's mark by colour and shape (an ink square where it belongs, an amber disc when it
  is elsewhere in the word, a sunken tile when it is not in it), a keyboard that keeps every
  letter's best mark, Ñ beside L in Spanish, and a hardware keyboard that types too.
- A word refused is said in one line above the keyboard, and the row shakes; a word found is
  greeted with «That’s the word.», then the card with the word as it is written, your words and
  the result to share.
- Every guess is saved as it is made: close the app in the middle of a game and it is there,
  where you left it.
- TalkBack reads each row as one sentence and each key with its mark; with animations off every
  movement is a short fade.
- The start page: today's word in each of the three languages, how each stands (waiting, under
  way, found in so many, missed), and one touch to play in another.
- Your streak in the game language, the days in a row with the word found on its own day.
- The calendar of the dailies: every day from the first word to today, found, missed, under way
  or not played, each drawn as the game draws a letter; touch a past day to play it from the
  archive. Days caught up later carry a dot, and count everywhere but in the streak.
- Free play, at the level you choose: Base, Intermediate, Advanced or Complete, each language its
  own. A word left halfway is waiting for you when you come back.
- Hard, from the start page, for the next game.
- When a day's word is over, play it again as practice: the day keeps its first result. When a
  free word is over, another one.
- The start page sees midnight come while it is open, and shows the new day's word.
- Long-press the icon for today's word in each language, straight to its game.
- Under the grid, the key of the three marks; the next tile to fill is framed; a game in Hard says
  so above its date.
- The first daily word is the one of 1 November 2026: the calendar already has days to play.
- The shared result marks a letter in its right place with a blue square, 🟦.
- In a language that is not your own, three hints behind the light bulb: the translation, the
  meaning or a letter in its place. Each costs an attempt, said before you take it, and is drawn
  in the row it spent.
- The end of a game explains the words in your language: the word's translation and meaning,
  and those of each word you tried.
- The notebook: every word a game hid, found or missed, and any word you tried that you choose to
  keep, with what it means, when you met it and how. Search it with or without accents, or look
  at one language at a time; it opens from the start page.
- Milestones: seven days in a row, a first word in another language, a hundred words in the
  notebook, the day's word in all three languages. Each is said once, on the card of the game
  that reached it.
- Your progress, from the start page: for each language, the daily word and free play apart, the
  share of words found, your streak and your longest, where your attempts went (your latest game
  marked), the games with hints and in Hard counted apart, and every milestone, with the day it
  was reached or how near it is.
- Your data: save a copy of your games, your notebook, your milestones and your choices to a file
  you keep where you like, and bring it back on another phone. A copy is read and checked whole,
  and shown with what it will replace, before anything changes.
- Share your result as a picture, the grid's shapes and colours without a letter, or as text; the
  sheet shows the picture as it will be sent.
- A first run: the name and three promises, the language you will play in (your own, or one to
  practise), the level to start from in another language, then a first game that teaches the
  rules as they happen, one sentence at a time, never a page of instructions. Skip it if you
  know the game.
- Settings, from the start page: how to play in three lines, with the first game to play again;
  the game language, free play's level and Hard; the app's language, through your phone's own
  picker; sounds, vibration and motion (as the phone, less or full); the theme, the dress, the
  typeface and your wallpaper's colours; your data; what the game sends (nothing), the licences
  and every source of the words, with their credits.
- Sounds, made by the game's own code: a soft tick for each key, a note for each tile as a row is
  revealed (your result plays as a short phrase), a word refused, a word found, a milestone.
  Silent whenever your phone is.
- A daily reminder, off until you turn it on: one quiet notification at the hour you choose, only
  while today's word is still to find, gone at midnight. A touch opens the word.
- A card for your home screen, «Today’s word»: each language’s word of today as the start page
  draws it (found, missed, under way or waiting) and your streak, in any size from one cell up.
  A touch on a language opens its word. It changes after every game and at midnight, never on a
  timer; hold it to choose what it stands on (light, dark, as the phone, or one of six colours)
  and how solid it is. Settings can add it for you.
- In the dark, a letter in its right place carries a light edge of its own blue, in the grid, on
  the keys, in the calendar and wherever a word found is drawn: on a dark page the ink blue no
  longer melts into the ground or into a letter that is not in the word.
- With TalkBack, the row being typed says where you are, "Row 3 of 6", and reads back every
  letter you type or delete; the first game's sentences say their numbers as each language does.
- A faster first launch: the start, the first run, the start page and the game come compiled
  with the app, rather than learnt by the phone over its first days.
